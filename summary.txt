World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine, Russia and the United States ended two days of talks in Abu Dhabi without a peace deal. Kyiv rejected a US proposal covering unoccupied parts of Donbas, but the sides agreed a large prisoner exchange, reported at about 1,000 prisoners each (BBC). On the battlefield, Ukraine said its forces damaged five oil storage tanks at Russia's Ust-Luga port on the Baltic, while Russian strikes hit six electricity substations and a market in Chornomorsk, killing three people and injuring 11 (Ukrainian General Staff).

World leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres warned that the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction" (AFP).

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said economic pressure on Iran was working and that major combat operations were no longer needed, but warned of force if Tehran crossed "certain red lines" (New York Times). The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in the United States and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford in England (Justice Department). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after threats against his life (New York Times).

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier in England on suspicion of spying for Russia (BBC). In Gaza, Israeli forces killed at least seven people, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said, and a 14-year-old boy was killed in an operation in the West Bank (BBC).

In Washington, House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth (NBC News). Twelve people died in two pile-ups in dense fog on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway (BBC). Delhi's air remained poor after Diwali, with the air quality index at 299 on Wednesday morning (Zark News).
